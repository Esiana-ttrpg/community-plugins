# Moon-Touched

**Moon-Touched** is the name families use when the Directorate's forms ask for something gentler than *curse bearer*. It is a lineage, not a species — inherited susceptibility passed through bloodlines that lived too close to lunar bindings when [[loc-lunar-city|Lunar City]] was still a promise instead of a pressure cooker. Moon-touched individuals do not glow in moonlight or sprout antlers in textbooks. They *register* the moon: eclipses as somatic events, sirens as triggers, reflections that arrive slightly out of phase with the bodies that cast them.

The condition is uneven. Some carriers feel only a seasonal melancholy the wellness office misdiagnoses. Others, especially after catastrophic rituals like the [[event-recent-eclipse|Recent Eclipse]], deteriorate quickly — tremors, prophetic recall, dreams of falling from heights that do not exist. [[family-ashvale|House Ashvale]] documented generations of variance in private ledgers the public archive will never see. [[family-shirogane|House Shirogane]] produced scholars who translated those ledgers into academic distance until a younger sister named Sora started writing eclipse intervals no textbook contained.

Known for curse bearing because the [[lore-moon-curse|Moon Curse]] does not strike randomly. It preferentially frays lines sworn to maintain bindings — [[family-ashvale|House Ashvale]]'s compact being the most famous, not the only one. Moon-touched heirs were once honored as living gauges: if they sickened, the binding needed repair. Now the Directorate calls it moon sickness in press releases and the [[org-eclipse-syndicate|Eclipse Syndicate]] calls it leverage.

In play, moon-touched identity is secrecy and cost. [[pc-ren|Ren Ashvale]] hides it to protect the coven's narrative. Sora cannot hide what she remembers. [[pc-yuki|Yuki Arden]] walks institutional corridors where moon-touched registration is a flag on a file she is not supposed to see. The ancestry ties directly to questions the coven cannot defer: whether the curse can be contained without sacrificing the people it marks, and whether binding oaths that saved a city ever had the right to ask that sacrifice in the first place.

The moon does not care about pedigree charts. It cares about who is still listening when the eclipse ends — and moon-touched listeners hear more than the city intends.
