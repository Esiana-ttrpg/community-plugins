# The First Binding

Before Lunar City had towers or transit lines, the founders raised a stone circle where [[loc-old-shrine]] now stands. Histories call it the First Binding — the night humans chose to **carry** the moon-curse instead of letting it pool in the bay and drown the first settlement.

Party-safe accounts describe a chorus of volunteers, a lunar keystone (later shaped into the idol the coven knows), and a charter that established shared guardianship. Academy plaques honor "the four quarters of the sky." Resistance songs are less polite: they count **five participants** standing shoulder to shoulder when the bind closed, each bearing a named duty in the old tongue.

On the shrine's weathered founding plaque — half buried behind modern railings — someone chiseled five names. The fourth from the left is legible. The fifth is not: gouged deep enough to splinter stone, then filled with mortar that has cracked again with age. Tour guides say vandalism. Elder binders say **correction**.

Fragments of the liturgy survive in [[lore-moon-curse]]: five bearers, one of whom must disappear so the others can live as ordinary girls. The founders' journals, where the party has seen them, disagree on whether the fifth walked away, was **removed**, or became the first Regent behind a mask.

What is not in dispute: the binding held for generations while the witness count in official records slowly shrank from five to four. Whatever happened to the fifth participant, the city built itself around their absence — annex rooms bricked over, songs shortened, eclipse reports templated to leave a blank line.

The First Binding is not ancient myth. It is the **terms of service** the coven is still failing to meet.
