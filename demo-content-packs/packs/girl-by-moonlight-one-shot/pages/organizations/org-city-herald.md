# City Herald Media

City Herald Media owns the skyline spike of [[loc-broadcast-tower]] and the habit of naming what [[loc-lunar-city]] would rather leave unnamed. Its evening broadcast still opens with weather and transit, but after the [[event-recent-eclipse]] the ad breaks grew longer and the investigative segments shorter — not from lack of stories, from lawyers seated beside producers. The Herald built its reputation on municipal accountability: zoning scandals, transit graft, the slow rename from Civic Watch Committee to [[org-lunar-directorate]]. Moon-curse coverage was once filed next to horoscopes. That taxonomy collapsed the night observatory lights returned.

The newsroom's sharpest edge is a **Herald reporter** who has covered [[org-shirogane-academy]] long enough to recognize performance from panic. She does not know the coven's true names, but she knows [[pc-mira]]'s handwriting on anonymous tip envelopes and she knows when campus security logs contradict the official "equipment failure" story. She wants the [[thread-city-secret]] because careers are made on stories cities deny — and because three missing students have mothers who still call the tip line.

Motivation inside the Herald splits by floor. Executives fear losing broadcast licenses if the Directorate labels eclipse panic as sedition. Reporters fear becoming stenographers for [[lore-directorate-secret]] redactions. The organization survives on tension between those fears: a leaked paragraph here, a pulled segment there, enough truth to keep credibility, enough restraint to keep transmitters hot.

Conflict is constant with [[org-lunar-directorate]] press officers who treat every question about [[loc-ruined-observatory]] as national security. [[quest-media-leak]] proved the Herald can be manipulated from inside and outside; completed does not mean resolved. The [[org-crescent-resistance]] distrusts cameras outright, yet sometimes needs the Herald's reach when a Syndicate recruiter must be named in daylight.

Future pressure arrives as [[event-future-convergence]] approaches. The reporter is circling Regent Vale's financial trails and the Directorate's sealed annex leases. If she publishes too soon, Vale's retaliation clock — the one [[quest-confront-regent]] warns about — starts early. If she waits, the city enters the next eclipse narrated by officials alone. The Herald's next headline will not just report the hidden war; it will choose who gets to define the war as crime, cult, or catastrophe.
