# Lunar City

Lunar City rose where planners promised a clean break from old superstitions — modular towers, glass transit, school campuses bright enough to pretend adolescence is safe. The founders buried older names beneath pavement and called the result progress. They were wrong in the specific way cities always are: the ground remembered. The [[lore-moon-curse]] predates the zoning maps, and every generation since has built institutions to manage what they refused to understand.

The region is an urban moon-curse hotspot because density amplifies secrecy. In a city this size, a girl can transform on a rooftop and be home by curfew if she knows which stairwells lack cameras. [[org-shirogane-academy]] anchors the eastern hills; [[loc-directorate-tower]] pins the civic center with calm broadcasts; [[loc-broadcast-tower]] repeats those broadcasts until they sound like truth. Between them run tram lines, shuttered arcades, and apartment blocks like [[loc-ashvale-apartment]] where families inherit oaths along with rent contracts.

History fractures at the [[event-recent-eclipse]]. Before it, Lunar City argued about budgets and festival seasons. After it, arguments turn to missing students, lights in [[loc-ruined-observatory]], and whether [[org-eclipse-syndicate]] was ever just a story. [[org-lunar-directorate]] calls the phenomenon managed. [[org-crescent-resistance]] calls that a lie. [[org-city-herald]] lives in the painful middle — enough evidence to frighten, never enough to force confession without retaliation.

Conflict here is not one battle but overlapping pressures. Regent Vale's agents hunt relics tied to [[obj-lunar-idol]]. Council President Elena negotiates curfews that protect some students and trap others. A Herald reporter chases threads the Directorate labels sedition. [[pc-hana]] crosses the city worrying about her sister **Sora**; [[pc-ren]] crosses it hiding a bloodline curse the Ashvale flat cannot contain alone.

Future pressure concentrates on [[event-future-convergence]]. Lunar City will not experience the next eclipse as weather. It will experience it as jurisdiction — who controls binding sites, who narrates transformation, who decides which girls are citizens and which are assets. The campaign region is small on a map and enormous in consequence: a single metropolitan wound where the hidden war already bleeds into morning commutes.
