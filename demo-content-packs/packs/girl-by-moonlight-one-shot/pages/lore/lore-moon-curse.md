# The Moon Curse

Lunar City teaches its children a gentle lie: the moon is a distant mirror, and eclipses are only astronomy. The coven knows the older story. The moon-curse is a wound in the sky that fell when the first city founders tried to **bind** lunar overflow into human vessels rather than let it flood the streets. Binding did not destroy the curse; it **scheduled** it — tidal grief, silver dreams, transformations that peel civilian life away from whoever carries too much reflected light.

Public shrines like [[loc-old-shrine]] were built as pressure valves. Offerings, songs, and synchronized oaths spread the weight across willing bearers. When the binders wrote the original liturgy — copied in fragments on academy walls and Resistance pamphlets — they named **five bearers**, not four: *one to hold the tide, one to read it, one to guard the gate, one to mourn it, one to disappear so the others could live as girls.* Modern textbooks trimmed the fifth line. The Resistance insists that was deliberate erasure, not poetic license.

The curse predates the city's founding charter; even party-safe histories admit the anomaly maps are older than the Directorate. What most civilians never learn is that **bearers inherit by witness**, not blood alone. You do not choose the moon; you are **present** when it breaks through — eclipse nights, shrine failures, moments when someone nearby should have died and did not. That is why the recent eclipse mattered. That is why Hana's sister sleeps with the curtains open.

Symptoms accumulate quietly. Hair that shines under streetlamps. Dreams of drowning in someone else's memory. A magical form that feels more honest than the face shown at school. Left unmanaged, the curse **settles** — not into one girl, but into the district around her: flickering lights, compulsive nocturnal wandering, pets howling at nothing. The Directorate calls these "atmospheric events." The Syndicate calls them **harvest windows**.

The failed containment rite at the shrine did not create the curse. It **reopened** a channel the founders had nailed shut with the lunar idol as keystone. Without that relic, the old binding text is only words — and words that still count five mouths when the coven has four.

Party-known remedies are partial. Shared oaths buy time. Anchor objects — especially idol-touched stone — calm surges. Distance from ley scars helps, which is why the academy sits where it sits and why the ruined observatory is forbidden ground. There is no cure in any pamphlet Ren has smuggled home.

What the coven suspects but cannot yet prove: someone in the founding generation **refused** the fifth seat, or was made to vanish from it, and the curse has been collecting interest on that absence ever since. Until the witness count matches the binding text again — five, not four — Lunar City will keep waking to eclipses that feel like accusations.
